Orvanna

Compensation plan brief

The Orvanna Builder Plan

A unilevel spine carrying two additive layers that pay for developing leaders, funded entirely out of money the existing plan already leaves unpaid.

Prepared 2026-08-17. Written to be read on its own, with no website and no system access. Every figure traces to the source table at the end.

Status, first, because it decides how you read everything else

One plan has run every month for six months and produced member statements. A different, larger plan has never been run for anybody at all. They share a spine. That sentence is the whole document in miniature, so it goes first.

And one fact frames both of them: Orvanna is a demonstration company. No money has been paid to anyone, by either plan. The orders behind every run were real orders placed through the live storefront using sandbox test cards, so the volume is genuine and the arithmetic ran on it, but no funds move at any point in the system.

The caveat that rides on every measured number here

The laboratory replays orders that really happened, under different rules. It shows exactly how pay would be redistributed. It cannot show that anybody would have behaved differently. Every claim in this brief about incentive is a claim about the shape of the payout, not a measured behavioural response. Separately, the modelled population grows steadily and almost never cancels, so it cannot certify how volatile real checks would be.

Why the modelled status is not a hole in the argument. The two new layers are additive, and they are funded entirely by money the live plan already leaves unpaid. Nobody's existing pay moves to fund them. That is a release test that was run, not a claim that was made: on the same month of orders the modelled plan's level-pay lines were compared against the live plan's lines in both directions, and there were zero differences across 1,630 lines totalling 13,434.00.

Acronym key

Sales Volume (SV), Commissionable Volume (CV), Personal Volume (PV), Team Volume (TV), Multi-Level Marketing (MLM), Quality Assurance (QA). This brief spells the terms out in full wherever it can, and uses the short forms only inside tables where space demands it.

Part one: the whole plan on one page

Sales Volume is the dollar volume of product bought by you and your customers in a month. Commissionable Volume is 80 percent of Sales Volume, and it is the only number any percentage is ever applied to. You are ACTIVE in any month your Sales Volume is at least 100.00, which is one subscription, or your customers' orders. A LEG is one person you personally sponsored plus everyone under them. A leg is ACTIVE when the person at its head is active.

1. Base pay runs today

You earn a percentage of the Commissionable Volume of each person in your organisation, by their level below you: 10, 5, 5, 3 and 2 percent through five levels. Your rank sets how deep you are paid. Two or more active legs makes you a Builder, paid two levels. Larger teams unlock Leader, Director and Executive at three, four and five levels.

2. The Builder override new

Any month somebody in your organisation holds Builder rank, the person who developed them earns 1.5 percent of that Builder's whole group volume, and the developer one generation further up earns 1.0 percent of the same thing. Nobody ever loses volume when somebody below them ranks up. Developing a Builder is pure addition to your check.

3. The second-team bonus new

Rank your legs by volume. If at least two of your legs are active, you earn 2.0 percent of your second-biggest leg's volume, stepping to 2.3 percent with three active legs and 2.5 percent with four or more. One giant leg pays nothing extra, on purpose. The plan pays you to build a second real team, and then a third.

4. Fresh every month

Everything above recomputes each month from that month's actual orders. Nothing is banked, nothing is grandfathered, nothing carries forward. If a Builder below you lapses, your override on them stops that month.

5. The 20 percent promise

No order ever pays out more than 20 percent of its price, enforced order by order inside the arithmetic itself. Your base pay is never reduced by this. When an order reaches its cap, the two bonus layers on that order share what remains, so a bonus line can pay less than its listed rate. In a typical modelled month about one order in eight is at its cap, and every reduced line on a statement must print what it claimed, what it paid, and why.

What never pays
Recruiting: not one dollar in this plan is paid for signing somebody up, and every payment is computed from product volume.  Your own purchases: they keep you active, they never earn you commission and never buy you a rank.  One giant leg.  Shell accounts: the bonus counts only ACTIVE legs, meaning real people with real 100.00 Sales Volume months.

Part two: the evidence

1. The headline number, and the four numbers it is never quoted without

The plan is supposed to pay for development. Here is whether it does, against the fairest available control: people with the same personal volume, differing only in whether they developed a Builder. Modelled March, 1,001 members, at the rates of record.

The two new layers are 22.9 percent of total payout, 3,983.12 of 17,417.12. That figure is never shown alone either. On its own it measures only that two layers exist and that the apex of the tree captures them.

Read together, these numbers say something specific: the premium on development is enormous and real, and it is concentrated today in a very small number of people, because a very small number of people have done that work.

2. What this plan does not do, said before anybody else says it

It does not pay more people. Modelled March: the live plan pays 206 members of 1,001, the Builder plan pays 207. One more. A different plan family run through the same laboratory, stairstep breakaway, pays 448. Concentration is slightly higher than the live plan, not lower.

3. Why it is shaped this way: proven parts, each with its worst habit removed

Nothing here is invented mathematics. Every mechanism has decades of industry service. What is new is the assembly, and specifically that each part arrives with its best-known failure mode structurally absent rather than forbidden by policy.

The four borrowed mechanisms and the ceiling
The partWhere it comes fromIts known failure modeRemoved how
Layer 1
the unilevel spine		 The industry's most common chassis, because it is the easiest plan to explain and the hardest to game. Passivity toward development. It pays on whatever depth happens to exist and pays nothing extra for turning a recruit into a leader. Layers 2 and 3 exist to pay for development explicitly. The spine itself is untouched, which keeps the explainability.
Layer 2
generation overrides		 The core of generational and breakaway plans, the oldest plan family in this industry. Volume theft. In a classic stairstep breakaway, promoting a leader removes their whole group from your volume, so the plan punishes the promotion it claims to want and the field learns to hold its best people down. No breakaway, stated as a rule: no volume ever leaves anybody's group. Developing a Builder is pure addition with zero subtraction, and there is no rank a downline can reach that costs an upline a cent.
Layer 3
lesser-leg pay		 Binary's engine. Paying on the weaker leg is the industry's most effective known driver of balance, which is why binaries dominate the fast-growth segment. Two of them: placement gaming, meaning spillover lotteries and purchased positions, and a runaway liability profile with carryover banking. No placement tree exists here. Legs are the real sponsorship genealogy, so there is nothing to place and no spillover to lottery, and nothing carries over. Liability is bounded twice: the bonus is junior in the waterfall and under the per-order cap.
The leg-count step Standard leadership-bonus structure, used by leadership pools and car programs for decades. Cliff effects. Big steps at magic numbers produce month-end buying to cross a threshold. The steps are deliberately shallow, 1.00 / 1.15 / 1.25, each one requires another qualified human rather than a purchase, and the arithmetic in section 8 shows the step never reverses the dilution, so there is no cliff worth buying.
The hard payout ceiling The discipline the company already lives by: the published promise that no order pays more than 20 percent of its price. Cost drift. Payout ratio creeping up as a field matures, discovered in the accounts months later, fixed by emergency plan changes that burn field trust. The ceiling is enforced per order, inside the calculation, not monitored after the fact. Drift is bounded by construction and plotted monthly.

Put plainly, for a hostile reader

A unilevel chassis carrying breakaway's development engine without the breakaway, binary's balance engine without the binary tree, a standard leadership step without the cliffs, all inside a solvency cap the company already promises.

4. Law A, decay, with both exhibits at the same size

Every layer recomputes from scratch each month from that month's orders and ranks. Nothing is banked or grandfathered. A Builder who lapses stops paying their upline that month. That property has a flattering face and an ugly one, and both are printed here at equal size.

Volatility, measured and caveated

Across 215 modelled member-month pairs there were 22 declines, four to seven members a month with falling override income, two drops of more than half, and one total wipe, which is exhibit two above. Because the modelled population grows steadily and almost never cancels, those months certify only that decay is attributable and countable. They cannot certify what real churn would look like.

5. The lapse-benefit inversion: the one claim that broke, disclosed with its bounds

When this plan was handed to a reviewer briefed to break it, one claim broke. The claim was that decay makes every upline the retention agent of everybody below them. Measured, that is true for the one or two Builder generations nearest a lapse, and it inverts above a binding ceiling.

The mechanism is Law B doing what it says. A lapse removes claims from pools that were oversubscribed, and the relief is shared out to everybody else claiming those same orders. Senior checks can rise when a deep member fails. The reviewer built it and ran it: nineteen members, eight nested Builders, one order whose pool was binding at a factor of 0.526315. One mid-stack Builder lapses to zero Sales Volume. The field loses 100.00 of Sales Volume, one hundred dollars of monthly order value rather than an abstract unit.

Three facts make it survivable, and each one is checkable

  1. It cannot be farmed. You cannot make somebody lapse. You can only decline to help them recover, and every attack built to construct this actively returned less than doing nothing.
  2. It is capped and it is never new money. The gain is exactly the freed share of pools that were already full: 7.44 on a 212.91 check, 3.5 percent, in a fixture built to maximise it. The company pays less on every lapse.
  3. The incentive still points the right way where anybody can act. The members nearest the lapse, the only ones positioned to notice and help, lose the most: 17.00 and 11.29 against a senior gain of 7.44.

It is not a corner of the parameter space. Every consecutive pair of runs counts the members whose pay rose because somebody below them lapsed, and sums the gain, as a standing measurement.

Why this is in the brief at all

A hostile reader finds this in one careful reading of the waterfall. A plan document that hands over its own worst finding, bounded and watched, is worth more than one that waits to be caught.

6. Law B, the waterfall: why the company's outlay is bounded by construction

Every order contributes a pool equal to 25 percent of its Commissionable Volume. Since Commissionable Volume is 80 percent of the price, and 0.25 x 0.80 = 0.20, that pool is exactly 20 percent of the money in the door: the same ceiling the company already publishes. The layers then draw from that pool in order of seniority.

Layer 2 draws from what is left. If the override claims on that order fit, they pay in full; otherwise every override claim on that order is scaled by the same factor. Layer 3 draws from what is left after that, by the same rule.

Where the money for the new layers comes from

All the team-building money in this plan is reclaimed breakage, and nothing else: unreachable levels, unqualified earners, depth gates. The factors are closed formulas over the complete claim set on each order, never a ledger drained in some sequence, so no ordering of members, claims or layers can change any answer.

And the case that blows up if it can

Take an order carrying 80.00 of Commissionable Volume, so a pool of 20.00, sitting under ten nested Builders, so override claims total two and a half times its whole pool. With a full five levels of qualified upline, level pay takes the entire 20.00, the remaining pool is zero and every one of those override claims pays 0.00. With only two levels of qualified upline, level pay takes 12.00, the remaining 8.00 is shared across 48.00 of claims at a factor of 0.166666, and the ten Builders collectively receive exactly 8.00. In both cases the order pays at most 20.00, to the cent. In the actual modelled census the worst real order carries override claims at 0.56 of its pool, and zero of 1,001 orders paid over pool.

Independent hostile cases. A separate reviewer built worse ones on purpose. In one the proration factor lands on a repeating decimal, 0.454545, and the paid total comes to 7.999992 against 8.000000 of remaining pool: strictly under, because every reduced amount is rounded down. In another the pool is exhausted entirely, the factor is 0.000000, every override and bonus claim on that order pays nothing, and the claims stay fully recorded and auditable.

7. How often the ceiling bites, and who feels it

An earlier draft of this plan predicted proration would be "rare and junior". The measurement kept "junior" and retired "rare". The word was changed rather than defended.

The statement rule, which is part of the plan

A reduced line is not recomputable by the person receiving it. It depends on other people's claims against shared orders, order by order, at six decimal places. One real example: a member's bonus line shows rate 2.0 percent and basis 120.00. Rate times basis is 2.40. The line pays 0.17, a 93 percent reduction, with nothing on the statement to explain it.

So the plan requires, as normative text with a release-gate condition attached, that every reduced line prints the amount claimed, the amount paid, and a one-line reason. A shrunken number a member cannot recompute is survivable only when the statement shows the claim, the payment, and the why.

The bonus falls as the step rises, because dilution beats the step at every count: fixed downline volume V across two equal legs pays 0.0100 x V, across three 0.0077 x V, across four 0.0063 x V. Splitting also forfeits overrides entirely.

The uncomfortable finding from the same test, published rather than buried

The attacker's own best outcome is the flat, undeveloped shape at 50.00, because the 10 percent frontline rate beats every development rate in the plan. The plan pays the field most for the developed shape, 79.20 against 50.00, and pays the individual most for the flat one. This plan does not reverse a unilevel's preference for width among your own recruits, and it must never be pitched as though it does.

Sandbagging the strong leg to inflate the second one

Base: legs of 240.00 and 120.00, bonus 2.40, total 38.40. Cut the strongest frontline from 300.00 to 100.00 Sales Volume: legs become 80.00 and 120.00, the second-ranked value falls to 80.00, bonus falls to 1.60, level pay falls from 36.00 to 20.00, total falls to 21.60. Removing volume from any leg can never raise the second-largest value, and nothing carries over, so volume withheld is pay lost permanently.

Funding a fake Builder

Buying rank and income with your own money

One member, Sales Volume 10,000.00, no downline. Total earned 0.00. Rank: Member. Own purchases buy qualification, the standard low gate, and nothing else. Builder requires two active legs, meaning two other people each reaching 100.00 Sales Volume that month. Leader and above require Team Volume, which excludes your own. And a 2,000.00 one-time order is recognised as 200 Personal Volume a month for ten months rather than 2,000 at once, so it cannot spike a month to grab a rank.

Waterfall arbitrage: none

Claims attach to orders by tree position, never by an earner's choice, so nobody can route a claim toward a richer pool. Proration factors are per-order facts, not a negotiable order of operations.

Regulatory smell test, checked against engine output rather than prose

On a full 1,001-member run: exactly four kinds of commission line exist and every one is computed from Commissionable Volume, which is product dollars. No line, rate or parameter anywhere pays on a count of recruits, enrollments or sign-ups. Zero lines pay anybody on their own volume as the source. Zero lines have a missing or non-positive basis. Zero orders exceeded the 20 percent ceiling in any run made, including every fixture built specifically to break it.

What survives after every attack fails

The intended behaviour: qualify yourself, keep two or more real legs active, develop Builders, and help them stay qualified, because decay re-prices all of it monthly. The best exploit anybody found is doing the work.

10. The calibration, including the part that missed

The rates were not chosen by taste. A budget was set first: the two new layers should land between 2.0 and 4.0 points of Commissionable Volume above the live plan's 14.6085 on a stated month. The draft rates were then measured against it and scaled once by a stated rule.

21.9712%
What the draft rates of 4, 2 and 5 percent paid. An increment of 7.3627 points, far outside the budget.
x 0.4075
The one-shot rule: a single common factor of 3.0 / 7.3627, giving 1.5, 1.0 and 2.0 percent, each rounded to the nearest half of a tenth of a percent.
18.9399%
The calibrated result. An increment of 4.3314 points, which is 0.33 of a point ABOVE the budget's ceiling.

The miss has a cause worth naming: proration relief is not linear. As rates shrink, fewer orders reach their cap, with orders carrying a binding override factor falling from 333 to 120, so the payout shrinks more slowly than the rates do.

That residue was accepted and displayed rather than tuned away

Iterating the rates to convergence would have been solving a fixed point against the ceiling's own proration, which is fitting a number rather than measuring one. The budget was a budget, not a law, and the miss is printed here for the same reason the inversion is.

Two more things the calibration produced, both published:

  • A prediction of ours was falsified and corrected. The specification expected the real population to be sparser in nested Builders than the small teaching fixture, so the increment should have landed lower. It landed far higher. The sentence was corrected to the measured truth rather than defended.
  • A counter-intuitive redistribution. Shrinking the senior rates made the junior layer's total pay rise, from 287.78 to 367.49, because smaller senior claims leave the junior class more room under the ceiling. That was verified independently as pure waterfall arithmetic, not a defect.

11. How this was checked before anybody saw it

The plan was not presentable until three independent gates passed it, each equal in standing.

Arithmetic gate: pass

An independent implementation recomputed the entire 1,001-member month from raw data with its own level map, its own ranks, its own group volumes, its own override walk, its own leg ranking, its own per-order claims and its own truncated factors, and matched the engine on every line in both directions: zero differences on level pay, zero on overrides, zero on bonuses, zero invariant violations across 1,001 orders, and the same total of 17,417.12 to the cent. Zero high-severity findings. That gate also built three hostile cases of its own design, including the repeating-decimal factor and the fully exhausted pool described in section 6, and the ceiling held in all three.

Quality gate: pass

Passed.

Red-team gate: initially NOT PRESENTABLE, then pass

A reviewer briefed as a veteran compensation consultant and told to make the plan fail before anybody saw it ran eight lines of attack in the live engine. Seven held. One broke a claim the plan document made, which is the inversion in section 5 above. The reviewer also caught the document quoting rates its own parameter table had superseded, and required three number-pairings for any presentation, which this brief follows.

The plan was amended the same day, the reviewer re-inspected item by item against their own required list, and the gate flipped to pass.

Two further properties on the record. Two independent implementations of this plan must agree to the cent, and they do. And the same month computed twice produces the same ordered set of lines and the same digest.
