Compensation plan brief

The Orvanna Builder Plan

A unilevel spine carrying two additive layers that pay for developing leaders, funded entirely out of money the existing plan already leaves unpaid.

Prepared 2026-08-17. Reads on its own, with no website and no system access. Every figure traces to the source table at the end.

Status, first, because it decides how you read everything else

One plan has run every month for six months and produced member statements. A different, larger plan has never been run for anybody. They share a spine.

Orvanna is a demonstration company. No money has been paid to anyone, by either plan. The orders behind every run were real orders placed through the live storefront using sandbox test cards, so the volume is genuine and the arithmetic ran on it, but no funds move at any point in the system.

The caveat that rides on every measured number here

The laboratory replays orders that really happened, under different rules. It shows exactly how pay would be redistributed. It cannot show that anybody would have behaved differently. Every claim about incentive is a claim about the shape of the payout, not a measured behavioural response. The modelled population also grows steadily and almost never cancels, so it cannot certify how volatile real checks would be.

Acronym key

Sales Volume (SV), Commissionable Volume (CV), Personal Volume (PV), Team Volume (TV), Multi-Level Marketing (MLM), Quality Assurance (QA).

Part one: the whole plan on one page

Sales Volume is the dollar volume of product bought by you and your customers in a month. Commissionable Volume is 80 percent of Sales Volume, and it is the only number any percentage is ever applied to. You are ACTIVE in any month your Sales Volume is at least 100.00. A LEG is one person you personally sponsored plus everyone under them, and it is ACTIVE when the person at its head is active.

1. Base pay runs today

A percentage of the Commissionable Volume of each person in your organisation, by their level below you: 10, 5, 5, 3 and 2 percent through five levels. Rank sets how deep you are paid. Two or more active legs makes you a Builder, paid two levels. Larger teams unlock Leader, Director and Executive at three, four and five levels.

2. The Builder override new

Any month somebody in your organisation holds Builder rank, the person who developed them earns 1.5 percent of that Builder's whole group volume, and the developer one generation further up earns 1.0 percent of the same thing. Nobody loses volume when somebody below them ranks up. Developing a Builder is pure addition.

3. The second-team bonus new

Rank your legs by volume. With at least two active legs you earn 2.0 percent of your second-biggest leg, stepping to 2.3 percent with three and 2.5 percent with four or more. One giant leg pays nothing extra, on purpose.

4. Fresh every month

Everything recomputes each month from that month's actual orders. Nothing is banked, grandfathered or carried forward. If a Builder below you lapses, your override on them stops that month.

5. The 20 percent promise

The monthly commission run never pays out more than 20 percent of an order's price, enforced inside the arithmetic itself, across your whole month of orders taken together. One carve-out: once Instant Payout is built, a first order will additionally carry a payment made on the day, so a first order can exceed 20 percent in total. Instant Payout is not built and pays nothing today.

Your base pay is never reduced by the ceiling. When a month reaches its cap the two bonus layers share what remains, so a bonus line can pay less than its listed rate. In a typical modelled month about one member-month in four with any volume is reduced, and every reduced line on a statement must print what it claimed, what it paid, and why.

What never pays
Recruiting: not one dollar is paid for signing somebody up; every payment is computed from product volume.  Your own purchases: they keep you active, they never earn you commission and never buy you a rank.  One giant leg.  Shell accounts: the bonus counts only ACTIVE legs, meaning real people with real 100.00 Sales Volume months.

2. The development premium, with its sample sizes and its companions

Does the plan pay for development? Against the fairest control available: members with the same personal volume, differing only in whether they developed a Builder. Modelled March, at the rates of record, over members who qualified that month.

Read together: the premium on development is enormous and real, it is measured on eight members per band, and it is concentrated today in a very small number of people, because a very small number of people have done that work.

Put plainly, for a hostile reader

A unilevel chassis carrying breakaway's development engine without the breakaway, binary's balance engine without the binary tree, a standard leadership step without the cliffs, all inside a solvency cap the company already promises.

5. Law A, decay, with both exhibits at the same size

Every layer recomputes from scratch each month. A Builder who lapses stops paying their upline that month. That property has a flattering face and an ugly one, and both are printed here at equal size.

6. The lapse-benefit inversion: the one claim that broke

Handed to a reviewer briefed to break it, one claim broke: that decay makes every upline the retention agent of everybody below them. Measured, that is true for the one or two Builder generations nearest a lapse and it inverts above a binding ceiling, because a lapse frees claims from pools that were oversubscribed and the relief is shared out. Nineteen members, eight nested Builders, one member-month whose pool was binding at a factor of 0.526315. One mid-stack Builder lapses to zero Sales Volume.

Why this is in the brief at all

A hostile reader finds this in one careful reading of the waterfall. A plan document that hands over its own worst finding, bounded and watched, is worth more than one that waits to be caught.

7. Law B, the waterfall: why the outlay is bounded by construction

Every member-month contributes a pool of 25 percent of its Commissionable Volume. Since Commissionable Volume is 80 percent of the price, and 0.25 x 0.80 = 0.20, that pool is exactly 20 percent of the money in the door. The layers draw from it in order of seniority.

Layer 2 draws from what is left; if the override claims fit they pay in full, otherwise every override claim on that member-month is scaled by the same factor. Layer 3 draws from what is left after that, by the same rule.

Where the money for the new layers comes from

All the team-building money in this plan is reclaimed breakage, and nothing else: unreachable levels, unqualified earners, depth gates. The factors are closed formulas over the complete claim set on each member-month, never a ledger drained in some sequence, so no ordering of members, claims or layers can change any answer.

And the cases built to blow it up

  • Ten nested Builders over a pool of 20.00, so override claims total two and a half times the whole pool. With five levels of qualified upline, level pay takes the entire 20.00 and every override claim pays 0.00. With two levels, level pay takes 12.00 and the remaining 8.00 is shared across 48.00 of claims at a factor of 0.166666, so the ten Builders collectively receive exactly 8.00. Both cap at 20.00, to the cent.
  • A repeating decimal. An independent reviewer forced a factor of 0.454545; the paid total came to 7.999992 against 8.000000 of remaining pool, strictly under, because every reduced amount is rounded down.
  • An exhausted pool. Factor 0.000000, every override and bonus claim pays nothing, and the claims stay fully recorded and auditable.
  • In the actual census the worst real member-month carries override claims at 0.56 of its pool, and zero of the 1,001 member-months paid over pool.

8. How often the ceiling bites, and how deep

The statement rule, which is part of the plan

A reduced line is not recomputable by the person receiving it: it depends on other people's claims against shared member-months, at six decimal places. One real example: a bonus line shows rate 2.0 percent and basis 120.00, so rate times basis is 2.40, and the line pays 0.17, a 93 percent cut, with nothing on the statement to explain it.

So the plan requires, as normative text with a release-gate condition attached, that every reduced line prints the amount claimed, the amount paid, and a one-line reason.

The bonus falls as the step rises, because dilution beats the step at every count: fixed downline volume V across two equal legs pays 0.0100 x V, across three 0.0077 x V, across four 0.0063 x V. Splitting also forfeits overrides entirely.

The uncomfortable finding from the same test, published rather than buried

The attacker's own best outcome is the flat, undeveloped shape at 50.00, because the 10 percent frontline rate beats every development rate in the plan. The plan pays the field most for the developed shape, 79.20 against 50.00, and pays the individual most for the flat one. This plan does not reverse a unilevel's preference for width among your own recruits, and it must never be pitched as though it does.

Sandbagging the strong leg to inflate the second one

Base: legs of 240.00 and 120.00, bonus 2.40, total 38.40. Cut the strongest frontline from 300.00 to 100.00 Sales Volume: legs become 80.00 and 120.00, the second-ranked value falls to 80.00, bonus falls to 1.60, level pay falls from 36.00 to 20.00, total falls to 21.60. Removing volume from any leg can never raise the second-largest value, and nothing carries over.

Funding a fake Builder, and the bound on self-funding

Buying rank and income with your own money

One member, Sales Volume 10,000.00, no downline. Total earned 0.00. Rank: Member. Builder requires two active legs, meaning two other people each reaching 100.00 Sales Volume that month. Leader and above require Team Volume, which excludes your own. And a 2,000.00 one-time order is recognised as 200 Personal Volume a month for ten months rather than 2,000 at once, so it cannot spike a month to grab a rank.

Waterfall arbitrage: none

Claims attach to member-months by tree position, never by an earner's choice, so nobody can route a claim toward a richer pool. Proration factors are per-source facts, not a negotiable order of operations.

Regulatory smell test, checked against engine output rather than prose

On the full 1,001-member-month run: exactly four kinds of commission line exist and every one is computed from Commissionable Volume, which is product dollars. No line, rate or parameter anywhere pays on a count of recruits, enrollments or sign-ups. Zero lines pay anybody on their own volume as the source. Zero lines have a missing or non-positive basis. Zero member-months exceeded the 20 percent ceiling in any run made, including every fixture built specifically to break it.

What survives after every attack fails

Qualify yourself, keep two or more real legs active, develop Builders, and help them stay qualified, because decay re-prices all of it monthly. The best exploit anybody found is doing the work.

The cost also rises as a field matures, because a more developed field produces more Builders and more balanced legs, which is the entire point.

11. The calibration, including the part that missed

The rates were not chosen by taste. A budget was set first: the two new layers should land between 2.0 and 4.0 points of Commissionable Volume above the live plan's 14.6085. The draft rates were measured against it and scaled once by a stated rule.

21.9712%
What the draft rates of 4, 2 and 5 percent paid. An increment of 7.3627 points, far outside the budget.
x 0.4075
The one-shot rule: a single common factor of 3.0 / 7.3627, giving 1.5, 1.0 and 2.0 percent, each rounded to the nearest half of a tenth of a percent.
18.9399%
The calibrated result. An increment of 4.3314 points, which is 0.33 of a point ABOVE the budget's ceiling.

The miss has a cause worth naming: proration relief is not linear. As rates shrink, fewer member-months reach their cap, with those carrying a binding override factor falling from 333 to 120, so the payout shrinks more slowly than the rates do.

The residue was accepted and displayed rather than tuned away

Iterating to convergence would have been solving a fixed point against the ceiling's own proration, which is fitting a number rather than measuring one. The budget was a budget, not a law, and the miss is printed here for the same reason the inversion is.

A prediction of ours was falsified. The specification expected the real population to be sparser in nested Builders than the small teaching fixture, so the increment should have landed lower. It landed far higher, and the sentence was corrected to the measured truth rather than defended.

A counter-intuitive redistribution. Shrinking the senior rates made the junior layer's total pay rise, from 287.78 to 367.49, because smaller senior claims leave the junior class more room under the ceiling. Verified independently as pure waterfall arithmetic, not a defect.
