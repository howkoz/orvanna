## 7. NEW PAGE SECTION 16: "Layer two: the Builder override"

Kicker: "Section sixteen".

Lead:

> The plan in part one is passive about leadership. It pays you on whatever depth
> happens to exist below you, and it pays you nothing extra for the work of
> turning a recruit into somebody who can build. Layer 2 is the answer to that,
> and it is the oldest mechanism in this industry.

**What gets paid.**

> Your **group volume** is your own Commissionable Volume plus the Commissionable
> Volume of everybody in your entire downline, at every depth, with nothing
> excluded.
>
> Any month a Conductor in your organisation holds Builder rank or higher, that
> Builder's whole group volume becomes a basis. The qualified Conductor above
> them with no other Builder in between earns **generation one**, 1.5 percent of
> that group volume. The next qualified Conductor above that, with one Builder
> boundary crossed, earns **generation two**, 1.0 percent of the same group
> volume. There is no generation three.

**The rule that makes this different from the classic version.**

> In the plan family this mechanism comes from, promoting somebody removes their
> whole group from your volume. The plan punishes the promotion it says it wants,
> and the field learns to hold its best people down. That is called a breakaway,
> and this plan does not have one. **No volume ever leaves anybody's group, at
> any rank, ever.** A nested Builder's group stays inside every group that
> encloses it. Developing a Builder is pure addition to your check with zero
> subtraction anywhere, and there is no rank a Conductor below you can reach that
> costs you a cent of volume.
>
> Stated honestly, because it is the cost of that choice: because groups nest,
> one order's volume can back override claims for several Conductors at once.
> Layer 2 is therefore not bounded by its own rates. It is bounded by Law B, in
> the section on Law B, which is where all the bounding in this plan happens.

**How the two generations are found: the walk.**

> Start at the Builder. Walk up the sponsorship chain toward the top, carrying a
> counter that starts at zero. At each Conductor you meet, first check whether
> they earn, then check whether they are a boundary.
>
> 1. If they are qualified and the counter is zero and generation one is not yet
>    assigned, they earn generation one.
> 2. Otherwise, if they are qualified and the counter is one and generation two is
>    not yet assigned, they earn generation two.
> 3. Then, if that same Conductor holds Builder rank or higher, add one to the
>    counter.
> 4. Stop when the counter reaches two, or at the top of the tree.

Three consequences worth stating, in a list:

- Generation one usually goes to the Builder's own sponsor, which is the case
  everybody pictures.
- If the sponsor is not qualified that month, the walk passes over them without
  counting them, and generation one goes further up. An unqualified Conductor
  neither earns nor blocks.
- One Conductor can collect generation one on one Builder and generation two on a
  deeper Builder in the same month. They are separate walks.

**A note on who can earn it**, in a panel:

> You do not have to be a Builder yourself to earn a Builder override. You have to
> be qualified, and there has to be a Builder below you. The walk already demands
> that somebody did the development work; adding a rank test on the earner would
> gate the same behavior twice. This is a decision, recorded as an open question,
> not an oversight.

- The node directly above: "qualified, counter is 0 -> earns GENERATION ONE, 1.5

## 8. NEW PAGE SECTION 17: "Layer three: the second-team bonus"

Kicker: "Section seventeen".

Lead:

> The most effective known way to make a compensation plan reward teamwork is to
> pay people on their weaker side rather than their stronger one. It is why
> binary plans dominate the fast-growth end of this industry. It also brings the
> two worst gaming problems in the industry with it: strategic placement in an
> artificial tree, and volume banked and carried forward. Layer 3 takes the
> engine and leaves both problems behind, because there is no artificial tree in
> this plan to place anybody in, and nothing carries forward.

**What gets paid.**

> A **leg** is one Conductor you personally sponsored plus that person's entire
> downline. A leg's volume is the Commissionable Volume of everybody in it,
> whether they are qualified or not, because volume is volume. A leg is
> **active** when the Conductor at its head is qualified this month, which is the
> same definition part one already uses.
>
> Rank your legs by volume, highest first. If you are qualified and at least two
> of your legs are active, you earn a bonus on the volume of your
> SECOND-strongest leg. The rate is 2.0 percent with two active legs, 2.3 percent
> with three, and 2.5 percent with four or more.

**One giant leg pays nothing extra, and that is the point**, in a lit note:

> With one leg there is no second leg, so there is no basis and no bonus. With two
> legs of wildly different sizes, the bonus is computed on the smaller one. The
> plan will not pay you more for making one leg enormous. It will pay you for
> building a second real team, and then a third. That is the entire design intent
> of this layer, said plainly.

**Two details a careful reader will ask about**, in a small panel:

> Legs are ranked by volume across ALL of your legs, while eligibility and the
> rate step count only ACTIVE legs. So a Conductor with two active legs and a
> third, fat, inactive leg is paid on whichever leg is second by volume. Ties are
> broken by the identifier of the Conductor at the head of the leg, so the answer
> is the same every time the month is computed.
>
> The rate steps are deliberately shallow. Large steps at magic numbers are what
> produce end-of-month buying to cross a threshold, and the section on what we attacked shows the
> arithmetic of why these steps are never worth buying: splitting volume across
> more legs loses more to dilution than the step gives back, at every count.

## 9. NEW PAGE SECTION 18: "Law A: nothing carries over"

> Every layer recomputes from scratch each month, from that month's orders and
> that month's ranks. There is no banking, no carryover, no highest-ever title, no

> boundaries are recomputed monthly. Legs are re-ranked monthly, so the

**What that is worth, exactly.** Two exhibits, side by side, both required, in
that order.

**Exhibit one, the acceptance fixture: the loss is exactly the override, to the
cent.**

> Four Conductors. A sponsors B. B sponsors C and D. Everybody holds one domain
> agent at $100.00, so everybody has Sales Volume 100.00 and Commissionable
> Volume 80.00, and everybody is qualified.
>
> Month one: B has two active legs, so B holds Builder. B's group volume is 80 +
> 80 + 80 = 240.00. A is a Member and reaches one level, so A's level pay reaches
> B and stops. A's check is 8.00 of level pay plus a generation one override on
> B's group.
>
> Month two: C cancels. B is still qualified but now has one active leg, so B is
> not a Builder. A's level pay is unchanged at 8.00, because B's own volume did
> not change. The override is gone.
>
> A's whole month-over-month loss is the override, to the cent, and the engine's
> attribution machinery assigns 100 percent of the change to the single cause.
> That is the retention agency made visible: A's check tells A precisely what
> keeping C active was worth.

| Conductor | Month one | Month two | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 8.00 of level pay, plus a Builder override of **3.60**, being 1.5 percent of B's group volume of 240.00 | 8.00 of level pay. The override is gone. | **-3.60, exactly the override** |
| B | 16.00 of level pay, plus a second-team bonus of 1.60 | 8.00 of level pay. The bonus is gone. | -9.60, being 8.00 of lost level pay on C plus the 1.60 bonus |

**Exhibit two, the same law with a real member, at the rates of record.**

> The Builder misses qualification for one month. A holiday, a card decline, a

> One qualifier this answer carries: that is the RANKING channel. The waterfall
> channel has the passive inversion disclosed in the section on Law A, which is
> bounded and
> watched there.

**"Monthly decay churns leaders who have one bad month."**

> Stated plainly: this version has no grace month, and the trade-off is real.
> Three things bound the damage.
>
> First, the qualification bar is low and wide. 100.00 Sales Volume is one domain
> agent, and customer volume counts toward it, so a working leader's
> qualification rarely turns on a single order of their own.
>
> Second, the loss lands FIRST on the one or two Builder generations nearest the
> lapse, and those are exactly the people positioned and now paid to intervene.
> The plan does not punish the bad month. It prices, for the sponsor, what
> preventing it was worth, to the cent. Above a binding ceiling the sign inverts,
> and that is disclosed in full in the section on Law A rather than left for a
> reader to
> find.
>
> Third, it is measured. Decay events and their summed effect are counted every
> run, so the churn cost is an observed number before this plan is presented, not
> a hope. Across 215 modelled member-month pairs there were 22 declines, four to
> seven Conductors a month with falling override income, two drops of more than
> half, and one total wipe, which is the exhibit shown in the section on Law A.
> And the
> caveat rides along: the modelled population grows steadily and almost never
> cancels, so those six months certify only that decay is attributable and
> countable, never that real churn would look like that.
>
> If observed decay proves too sharp, a one-month grace for Builder status is a
> single parameter, and adding it would be an amendment rather than a redesign.
> That decision is deliberately still open, and the section on what is not
> settled says so.

**"This double-pays the same volume."**

> Every override plan in this industry pays several people on one order. The
> honest question is whether the sum is bounded. The section on Law B follows one
> order all
> the way: three layers, four payments, 14.80 paid of a 20.00 ceiling, 5.20
> unspent. The plan never pays more than the 20 percent of revenue the company
> already promises today. It pays the same ceiling to more purposes.

**"The ceiling will reduce the field's checks and they will be furious."**

> An earlier draft predicted proration would be rare and junior. The measurement
> kept the word junior and retired the word rare: 120 of 1,001 orders reach their
> cap, and 35 of the 207 paid Conductors carry at least one reduced line. Those
> numbers, refreshed every run, are carried in any presentation of this plan.
>
> What makes it survivable is structural and stated in the section on Law B: the
> spine is
> never reduced, so nobody's live-plan-equivalent pay ever falls; and every
> reduced line must print what it claimed, what it paid, and why. A shrunken
> number a member cannot recompute is survivable only when the statement itself
> shows the claim, the payment, and the reason.

**"Your payout ratio will creep as the field professionalises. You have built cost
drift into the design, and it compresses margin exactly when you scale."**

> True, and answered structurally rather than denied. The drift is real, it is
> intended, it is plotted in the section on what the model measured at 0.22 points
> of Commissionable Volume a
> month at this maturity, and it is bounded above at 25 percent of Commissionable
> Volume per order by the same arithmetic that computes the payments. Every point
> of drift comes out of the gap between the 14.6 percent the plan pays today and
> the ceiling the price already carries. What maturity shrinks is the company's
> unpaid remainder, never the priced margin.

## 15. GLOSSARY: renumber, move last, add eleven terms

The glossary keeps every existing definition and its two-column layout. Change its
kicker from "Section fifteen" to "Section twenty-four" and move it after the new
part two sections. Keep the "Which document governs" note and the back-to-top
link at the very end.

Add these terms in alphabetical position among the existing ones.

- **Builder override** | A payment to a qualified Conductor above somebody who
  holds Builder rank this month, computed on that Builder's entire group volume.
  Part two of this brochure only. Modelled, not operating.
- **Cap, or ceiling** | The most one order can ever pay in commission: 25 percent
  of its Commissionable Volume, which is 20 percent of its price. Enforced inside
  the calculation, order by order.
- **Decay** | The rule that every layer recomputes from scratch each month from
  that month's orders and ranks, with nothing banked, carried forward, or
  grandfathered.
- **Generation** | In the Builder override, a step counted in Builders rather than
  in levels. Generation one is the first qualified Conductor above a Builder with
  no other Builder in between. Generation two is the next one up, past one Builder
  boundary.
- **Group volume** | A Conductor's own Commissionable Volume plus that of everybody
  in their entire downline at every depth. Groups nest: nothing ever leaves a
  group when somebody inside it ranks up.
- **Pool** | The money one order makes available for commission: 25 percent of its
  Commissionable Volume.
- **Proration** | Scaling every claim of one layer against one order down by the
  same factor, when those claims are larger than the pool that order has left.
  Level pay is never prorated.
- **Second-team bonus** | A bonus on the volume of a Conductor's second-strongest
  leg, paid only when at least two legs are active. Part two of this brochure
  only. Modelled, not operating.
- **Spine** | The name part two gives to layer 1, which is the live plan of part
  one, unchanged.
- **Structure-linked share** | The fraction of the total payout that comes from the
  Builder override and the second-team bonus rather than from level pay. Never
  quoted without the participation counts beside it.
- **Waterfall** | The order in which the layers draw from an order's pool: level
  pay first, overrides second, the second-team bonus last.
