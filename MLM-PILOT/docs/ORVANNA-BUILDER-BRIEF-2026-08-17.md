# The Orvanna Builder Plan: a brief

Prepared 2026-08-17. Readable on its own, without the website.

Acronym key: Sales Volume (SV), Commissionable Volume (CV), Personal Volume (PV),
Team Volume (TV), Multi-Level Marketing (MLM), Quality Assurance (QA).

---

## Status, first, because it decides how you read the rest

**What is operating.** Orvanna's live compensation plan is a unilevel: 10, 5, 5, 3
and 2 percent of a buyer's Commissionable Volume paid to the five people above
them, with paid depth set by rank and one qualification gate at 100.00 Sales
Volume a month. Twelve monthly commission runs have been computed on it, covering
February through July 2026. Every one of them carries version 1.2 or version 1.3
of that specification. Those runs produced statements.

**What is modelled.** The Builder plan described in this brief adds two layers on
top of that. It exists as a working implementation inside a compensation
laboratory, where 29 runs of it have been computed against recorded orders. It has
never been the live plan. It has never produced a statement. It has never paid one
dollar to one person. Every figure in this brief is a what-if computed from real
recorded volume under different rules.

**Why that is not a weakness in the argument.** Layer 1 of the Builder plan is not
a new version of the live plan. It IS the live plan, unchanged: the same rates,
the same five levels, the same rank-gated depth, the same gate. That is a release
test rather than a claim. On the same month of orders, the Builder plan's level-pay
lines were compared against the live plan's lines in both directions on earner,
source, level, basis, rate, and amount, across 1,630 lines totalling 13,434.00,
and there were zero differences. The two new layers are additive and are funded
entirely by money the live plan already leaves unpaid. Nobody's existing pay moves
to fund them.

**The caveat that rides on every measured number in this brief.** The laboratory
replays orders that really happened, under different rules. It shows exactly how
pay would be redistributed. It cannot show that anybody would have BEHAVED
differently. Every claim here about incentive is a claim about the shape of the
payout, not a measured behavioral response. Separately, the modelled population
grows steadily and almost never cancels, so it cannot certify how volatile real
checks would be.

## Part one: the plan on one page

**Words.** Sales Volume (SV) is the dollar volume of product bought by you and
your customers in a month. Commissionable Volume (CV) is 80 percent of Sales
Volume, and it is the only number any percentage is ever applied to. You are
ACTIVE in any month your Sales Volume is at least 100.00, which is one
subscription, or your customers' orders. A LEG is one person you personally
sponsored plus everyone under them. A leg is ACTIVE when the person at its head is
active.

**1. Base pay. This is the plan that runs today, unchanged.** You earn a
percentage of the Commissionable Volume of each person in your organisation, by
their level below you: 10, 5, 5, 3 and 2 percent through five levels. Your rank
sets how deep you are paid. Two or more active legs makes you a Builder, paid two
levels; larger teams unlock Leader, Director and Executive at three, four and five
levels.

**2. The Builder override. New.** Any month somebody in your organisation holds
Builder rank, the person who developed them earns 1.5 percent of that Builder's
WHOLE group volume, and the developer one generation further up earns 1.0 percent
of the same thing. Nobody ever loses volume when somebody below them ranks up.
Developing a Builder is pure addition to your check.

**3. The second-team bonus. New.** Rank your legs by volume. If at least two of
your legs are active, you earn 2.0 percent of your SECOND-biggest leg's volume,
stepping to 2.3 percent with three active legs and 2.5 percent with four or more.
One giant leg pays nothing extra, on purpose. The plan pays you to build a second
real team, and then a third.

**4. Fresh every month.** Everything above recomputes each month from that month's
actual orders. Nothing is banked, nothing is grandfathered, nothing carries
forward. If a Builder below you lapses, your override on them stops that month.

**5. The 20 percent promise.** No order ever pays out more than 20 percent of its
price, enforced order by order inside the arithmetic itself. Your BASE pay is
never reduced by this. When an order reaches its cap, the two bonus layers on that
order share what remains, so a bonus line can pay less than its listed rate. In a
typical modelled month about one order in eight is at its cap, and every reduced
line on a statement must print what it claimed, what it paid, and why.

**What never pays.** Recruiting: not one dollar in this plan is paid for signing
somebody up, and every payment is computed from product volume. Your own
purchases: they keep you active, they never earn you commission and never buy you
a rank. One giant leg. Shell accounts: the bonus counts only ACTIVE legs, meaning
real people with real 100.00 Sales Volume months.

## Part two: the evidence

### 1. The headline number, with its companions

The plan is supposed to pay for development. Here is whether it does, against the
fairest available control: people with the SAME personal volume, differing only in
whether they developed a Builder. Modelled March, 1,001 members, at the rates of
record.

| Sales Volume band | Mean pay with at least one personally sponsored Builder | Mean pay with none | Multiple |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100.00 to 149.99 | 52.47 | 6.66 | **7.9 times** |
| 150.00 to 299.99 | 725.57 | 7.90 | **92 times** |
| 300.00 and above | 955.31 | 11.77 | **81 times** |

**These rows are never quoted without these four numbers, and that is a rule of
this document, not a courtesy.**

- **24** members of 1,001 hold that position today, meaning they personally
  sponsored at least one Builder.
- **44** members earn any money at all from the two new layers.
- The top **five** of those 44 take 3,590.97 of the 3,983.12 the two layers paid,
  which is **90.2 percent of the entire structure budget**. The top ten take 94.2
  percent.
- The two new layers are **22.9 percent** of total payout (3,983.12 of
  17,417.12).

Read together they say something specific: the premium on development is enormous
and real, and it is concentrated today in a very small number of people, because a
very small number of people have done that work. The 22.9 percent alone would be
misleading. It measures that two layers exist and that the apex of the tree
captures them. It is never shown alone.

### 2. What this plan does not do, said before anybody else says it

It does not pay more people. Modelled March: the live plan pays 206 members of
1,001, the Builder plan pays 207. One more. A different plan family run through
the same laboratory, stairstep breakaway, pays 448. Concentration is slightly
HIGHER than the live plan, not lower: Gini 0.9687 against 0.9625, top-ten-percent
share 95.33 percent against 94.25 percent.

**The honest sentence, and the only one this plan will make: the same paid
population, a far deeper premium on development.** Anybody describing this plan as
broadening participation is describing a different plan.

### 3. Why it is shaped this way: proven parts, each with its worst habit removed

Nothing here is invented math. Every mechanism has decades of industry service.
What is new is the assembly, and specifically that each part arrives with its
best-known failure mode structurally absent rather than forbidden by policy.

| The part | Where it comes from | Its known failure mode | Removed how |
|---|---|---|---|
| Layer 1, the unilevel spine | The industry's most common chassis, because it is the easiest plan to explain and the hardest to game. | Passivity toward development. It pays on whatever depth happens to exist and pays nothing extra for turning a recruit into a leader. | Layers 2 and 3 exist to pay for development explicitly. The spine itself is untouched, which keeps the explainability. |
| Layer 2, generation overrides | The core of generational and breakaway plans, the oldest plan family in this industry. | Volume theft. In a classic stairstep breakaway, promoting a leader removes their whole group from your volume, so the plan punishes the promotion it claims to want and the field learns to hold its best people down. | No breakaway, stated as a rule: no volume ever leaves anybody's group. Developing a Builder is pure addition with zero subtraction, and there is no rank a downline can reach that costs an upline a cent. |
| Layer 3, lesser-leg pay | Binary's engine. Paying on the weaker leg is the industry's most effective known driver of balance, which is why binaries dominate the fast-growth segment. | Two: placement gaming, meaning spillover lotteries and purchased positions, and a runaway liability profile with carryover banking. | No placement tree EXISTS here. Legs are the real sponsorship genealogy, so there is nothing to place and no spillover to lottery, and nothing carries over. Liability is bounded twice: the bonus is junior in the waterfall and under the per-order cap. |
| The leg-count step | Standard leadership-bonus structure, used by leadership pools and car programs for decades. | Cliff effects. Big steps at magic numbers produce month-end buying to cross a threshold. | The steps are deliberately shallow, 1.00 / 1.15 / 1.25, each one requires another qualified human rather than a purchase, and the arithmetic in section 7 shows the step never reverses the dilution, so there is no cliff worth buying. |
| The hard payout ceiling | The discipline the company already lives by: the published promise that no order pays more than 20 percent of its price. | Cost drift. Payout ratio creeping up as a field matures, discovered in the accounts months later, fixed by emergency plan changes that burn field trust. | The ceiling is enforced per order, inside the calculation, not monitored after the fact. Drift is bounded by construction and plotted monthly. |

Put plainly for a hostile reader: a unilevel chassis carrying breakaway's
development engine without the breakaway, binary's balance engine without the
binary tree, a standard leadership step without the cliffs, all inside a solvency
cap the company already promises.

### 4. Law A, decay, with both exhibits

Every layer recomputes from scratch each month from that month's orders and ranks.
Nothing is banked or grandfathered. A Builder who lapses stops paying their upline
that month.

**Exhibit one, the tidy version: the loss is exactly the override, to the cent.**
Four members. A sponsors B. B sponsors C and D. Everybody at 100.00 Sales Volume,
80.00 Commissionable Volume, all qualified. In month one B has two active legs, so
B is a Builder with a group volume of 240.00. In month two C cancels, B has one
active leg and is no longer a Builder.

| Member | Month one | Month two | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 8.00 of level pay, plus a Builder override of **3.60**, being 1.5 percent of B's group volume of 240.00 | 8.00 of level pay. The override is gone. | **-3.60, exactly the override** |
| B | 16.00 of level pay, plus a second-team bonus of 1.60 | 8.00 of level pay. The bonus is gone. | -9.60, being 8.00 of lost level pay on C plus the 1.60 bonus |

A's level pay never reached C, so A's whole loss is the override, to the cent, and
the engine's attribution assigns 100 percent of it to the single cause. A's check
tells A precisely what keeping C active was worth.

*Rate note, so nobody has to catch it:* the figures above are stated at the rates
of record, the Builder override at 1.5 percent and the bonus at 2.0 percent. The
engine's acceptance fixture itself is RECORDED at the plan's earlier draft rates,
where the same structure produced 9.60 and 4.00, and it is kept that way
deliberately, because a fixture is a contract the engine is tested against and
every run stores the rates it used. What it proves, that the loss equals the
override exactly, does not depend on the rate.

**Exhibit two, the real version, at the rates of record.** In the modelled census
one member's entire check is a single Builder override of 7.80, being 1.5 percent
of a Builder's group volume of 520.00. That Builder is two hops down. The Builder
misses qualification for one month: a holiday, a card decline.

**That member's check goes from 7.80 to 0.00. One hundred percent of it, in one
month, because of somebody else's bad month.** Four other members' pay moved in
the same counterfactual: the Builder lost 33.20 of their own, and three uplines
lost between 4.00 and 12.40 of level pay.

That is not a curiosity. Today several modelled members are in the same fragile
class: in July, one member's check is 100 percent override lines, another's 90
percent, another's 87 percent. The countermeasure is part of the plan, not part of
the marketing: hold more than one Builder and more than one active leg.

**Volatility, measured and caveated.** Across 215 modelled member-month pairs there
were 22 declines, four to seven members a month with falling override income, two
drops of more than half, and one total wipe, which is the exhibit above. Because
the modelled population grows steadily and almost never cancels, those months
certify only that decay is attributable and countable. They cannot certify what
real churn would look like.

### 5. The lapse-benefit inversion: the one claim that broke, disclosed

When this plan was handed to a reviewer briefed to break it, one claim broke. The
claim was that decay makes every upline the retention agent of everybody below
them. Measured, that is true for the one or two Builder generations nearest a
lapse and it INVERTS above a binding ceiling.

The mechanism is Law B doing what it says. A lapse removes claims from pools that
were oversubscribed, and the relief is shared out to everybody else claiming those
same orders. Senior checks can rise when a deep member fails.

The reviewer built it and ran it: nineteen members, eight nested Builders, one
order whose pool was binding at a factor of 0.526315. One mid-stack Builder lapses
to zero Sales Volume. The field loses 100.00 of Sales Volume, one hundred real
dollars of monthly revenue.

| Member | Before | After the lapse | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| E, four Builder generations above the lapse | 212.91 | 220.35 | **+7.44** |
| B1 | 47.91 | 50.95 | +3.04 |
| B2 | 43.91 | 46.95 | +3.04 |
| B3 | 39.71 | 42.75 | +3.04 |
| B4 | 34.92 | 35.77 | +0.85 |
| B5, two above the lapse | 31.29 | 20.00 | -11.29 |
| B6, directly above the lapse | 28.60 | 11.60 | **-17.00** |
| B7, the member who lapsed | 25.89 | 0.00 | -25.89 |
| B8, below the lapse | 16.00 | 16.40 | +0.40 |
| **Company payout** | **481.14** | **444.77** | **-36.37** |

Six checks rose when the field lost a member. E's second-team bonus rose from 10.60
to 15.00 while the volume it was computed on FELL from 1,360.00 to 1,280.00: less
volume, more bonus, to the cent. The binding factor went from 0.526315 to 1, and
the ceiling held on both sides to the microcent, that order paying 19.999996 before
and 20.000000 after.

**Three facts make it survivable, and each one is checkable.**

One, it cannot be farmed. You cannot make somebody lapse. You can only decline to
help them recover, and every attack built to construct this actively returned less
than doing nothing.

Two, it is capped and it is never new money. The gain is exactly the freed share of
pools that were already full: 7.44 on a 212.91 check, 3.5 percent, in a fixture
built to maximise it. The company pays LESS on every lapse.

Three, the incentive still points the right way where anybody can act. The members
nearest the lapse, the only ones positioned to notice and help, lose the most: 17.00
and 11.29 against a senior gain of 7.44.

It is not a corner of the parameter space. At the rates of record, 120 of the 1,001
modelled orders have a binding pool, so the inversion is live in any presentation
month. It is therefore watched rather than assumed small: every consecutive pair of
runs counts the members whose pay rose because somebody below them lapsed, and sums
the gain, as a standing measurement.

*Why this is in the brief.* A hostile reader finds this in one careful reading of
the waterfall. A plan document that hands over its own worst finding, bounded and
watched, is worth more than one that waits to be caught.

### 6. Law B, the waterfall: why the company's outlay is bounded by construction

Every order contributes a pool equal to 25 percent of its Commissionable Volume.
Since Commissionable Volume is 80 percent of the price, and 0.25 x 0.80 = 0.20,
that pool is exactly 20 percent of the money in the door: the same ceiling the
company already publishes.

The layers then draw from that pool in order of seniority.

1. **Layer 1 draws first and is always paid in full.** The five level rates sum to
   10 + 5 + 5 + 3 + 2 = 25 percent of Commissionable Volume, and there is at most
   one person at each level, so layer 1 can never claim more than the pool holds.
   The spine always fits and is never reduced. That is what makes "layer 1
   unchanged" a fact rather than an intention.
2. **Layer 2 draws from what is left.** If the override claims on that order fit,
   they pay in full; otherwise every override claim on that order is scaled by the
   same factor.
3. **Layer 3 draws from what is left after that**, by the same rule.

**All the team-building money in this plan is reclaimed breakage, and nothing
else**: unreachable levels, unqualified earners, depth gates. The factors are
closed formulas over the complete claim set on each order, never a ledger drained
in some sequence, so no ordering of members, claims or layers can change any
answer.

**One order, followed all the way, at the rates of record.** One member's month:
Sales Volume 100.00, so 80.00 of Commissionable Volume, so a pool of 20.00. This
member sits inside a Builder's group and heads that Builder's second-strongest leg,
so all three layers claim at once.

| Layer | Who is paid | Arithmetic | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Layer 1 | the sponsor, level 1 | 0.10 x 80.00 | 8.00 |
| Layer 1 | the next person up, level 2 | 0.05 x 80.00 | 4.00 |
| Layer 2 | the Builder's generation one upline | 0.015 x 80.00 | 1.20 |
| Layer 3 | the Builder, on the second-strongest leg | 0.020 x 80.00 | 1.60 |
| | **Total paid on this order** | | **14.80** |
| | **The cap** | 0.25 x 80.00 | **20.00** |
| | **Unspent** | | **5.20** |

Three layers, four payments to three different people, and the order still pays
14.80 of a 20.00 ceiling. Every override plan in this industry pays several people
on one order. The only honest question is whether the sum is bounded, and here it
is bounded by the arithmetic that computes it.

**And the case that blows up if it can.** Take an order carrying 80.00 of
Commissionable Volume, so a pool of 20.00, sitting under ten nested Builders, so
override claims total two and a half times its whole pool. With a full five levels
of qualified upline, level pay takes the entire 20.00, the remaining pool is zero
and every one of those override claims pays 0.00. With only two levels of qualified
upline, level pay takes 12.00, the remaining 8.00 is shared across 48.00 of claims
at a factor of 0.166666, and the ten Builders collectively receive exactly 8.00. In
both cases the order pays at most 20.00, to the cent.

In the actual modelled census the worst real order carries override claims at 0.56
of its pool, and zero of 1,001 orders paid over pool.

**Independent hostile cases.** A separate reviewer built worse ones on purpose. In
one the proration factor lands on a repeating decimal, 0.454545, and the paid total
comes to 7.999992 against 8.000000 of remaining pool: strictly under, because every
reduced amount is rounded DOWN. In another the pool is exhausted entirely, the
factor is 0.000000, every override and bonus claim on that order pays nothing, and
the claims stay fully recorded and auditable.

### 7. How often the ceiling bites, and who feels it

Modelled March at the rates of record:

- **120 of 1,001 orders reach their cap, about 12 percent, roughly one in eight.**
- **35 of the 207 paid members, about 17 percent, carry at least one reduced
  line.**
- Total reduced away: 943.68, being 638.00 from layer 2 and 305.68 from layer 3,
  against 17,417.12 paid, about 5.4 percent of the run.
- Worst-affected member loses 324.14. Worst ratio is 21 percent of that member's
  claims.
- Every cent of it comes off layers 2 and 3. The spine is structurally untouchable,
  so no member's live-plan-equivalent pay is ever reduced.

An earlier draft of this plan predicted proration would be "rare and junior". The
measurement kept "junior" and retired "rare". The word was changed rather than
defended.

**The statement rule, which is part of the plan.** A reduced line is not
recomputable by the person receiving it. It depends on other people's claims
against shared orders, order by order, at six decimal places. One real example: a
member's bonus line shows rate 2.0 percent and basis 120.00. Rate times basis is
2.40. The line pays 0.17, a 93 percent reduction, with nothing on the statement to
explain it. So the plan requires, as normative text with a release-gate condition
attached, that **every reduced line prints the amount claimed, the amount paid, and
a one-line reason**. A shrunken number a member cannot recompute is survivable only
when the statement shows the claim, the payment, and the why.

### 8. What we attacked, and what it paid back

Every attack below was RUN in the engine at the rates of record, not argued on
paper.

**Splitting recruits across more legs to chase the rate step.** The same six
recruits, the same 700.00 of Sales Volume, three arrangements.

| Arrangement | Level pay | Overrides | Bonus | Attacker total | Paid to the field |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Two legs, nested, two Builders emerge | 32.00 | 7.20 | 4.80 at 2.0 percent | 44.00 | 79.20 |
| Three legs, one recruit under each | 36.00 | 0.00 | 3.68 at 2.3 percent | 39.68 | 63.68 |
| All six flat on the frontline | 48.00 | 0.00 | 2.00 at 2.5 percent | 50.00 | 50.00 |

The bonus FALLS as the step rises, because dilution beats the step at every count:
fixed downline volume V across two equal legs pays 0.0100 x V, across three 0.0077
x V, across four 0.0063 x V. Splitting also forfeits overrides entirely.

*The uncomfortable finding from the same test, published rather than buried.* The
attacker's own best outcome is the flat, undeveloped shape at 50.00, because the 10
percent frontline rate beats every development rate in the plan. The plan pays the
FIELD most for the developed shape, 79.20 against 50.00, and pays the individual
most for the flat one. This plan does not reverse a unilevel's width preference
among your own recruits, and it must never be pitched as though it does.

**Sandbagging the strong leg to inflate the second one.** Base: legs of 240.00 and
120.00, bonus 2.40, total 38.40. Cut the strongest frontline from 300.00 to 100.00
Sales Volume: legs become 80.00 and 120.00, the second-ranked value FALLS to 80.00,
bonus falls to 1.60, level pay falls 36.00 to 20.00, total falls to 21.60. Removing
volume from any leg can never raise the second-largest value, and nothing carries
over, so volume withheld is pay lost permanently.

**Funding a fake Builder.** Builder needs two active legs, so a puppet Builder
needs two more accounts at 100.00 Sales Volume: $300.00 a month of real money.

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Attacker's pay before | 8.00 |
| Level pay after | 24.00 |
| Generation one override after, 0.015 x group volume 240.00 | 3.60 |
| Bonus after | 1.60 |
| **Attacker's pay after** | **29.20** |
| **What the attack earned** | **21.20** |
| **What the attack costs, every month** | **300.00** |

**7.07 cents returned per attack dollar. A 92.9 percent loss, every month,
forever**, because decay makes the puppet a subscription rather than a purchase.
The floor under that is structural: no plan funded under a 20 percent revenue
ceiling can return more than 20 cents on a self-funded dollar, so every
self-funding attack on every layer loses at least 80 percent. This one loses 93.
Funding an artificial second leg is the same trade: $100.00 a month to return at
most 0.025 x 80.00 = 2.00, a 98 percent monthly loss.

**Buying rank and income with your own money.** One member, Sales Volume 10,000.00,
no downline. **Total earned 0.00. Rank: Member.** Own purchases buy qualification,
the standard low gate, and nothing else. Builder requires two ACTIVE legs, meaning
two other people each reaching 100.00 Sales Volume that month. Leader and above
require Team Volume, which excludes your own. And a $2,000.00 one-time order is
recognised as 200 Personal Volume a month for ten months rather than 2,000 at once,
so it cannot spike a month to grab a rank.

**Waterfall arbitrage: none.** Claims attach to orders by tree position, never by
an earner's choice, so nobody can route a claim toward a richer pool. Proration
factors are per-order facts, not a negotiable order of operations.

**Regulatory smell test, checked against engine output rather than prose, on a full
1,001-member run.** Exactly four kinds of commission line exist and every one is
computed from Commissionable Volume, which is product dollars. No line, rate or
parameter anywhere pays on a count of recruits, enrollments or sign-ups. Zero lines
pay anybody on their own volume as the source. Zero lines have a missing or
non-positive basis. Zero orders exceeded the 20 percent ceiling in any run made,
including every fixture built specifically to break it.

What survives after every attack fails is the intended behavior: qualify yourself,
keep two or more real legs active, develop Builders, and help them stay qualified,
because decay re-prices all of it monthly. The best exploit anybody found is doing
the work.

### 9. What it costs, and the drift, plotted rather than discovered

The live plan pays 14.6085 percent of Commissionable Volume on the modelled March.
This plan pays 18.9399 percent on the same month. Those two figures are printed
side by side everywhere this plan is discussed, and the phrase "size held equal" is
banned from every surface because it would not be true.

The cost also RISES as a field matures, because a more developed field produces
more Builders and more balanced legs, which is the entire point.

### 10. The calibration, including the part that missed

The rates were not chosen by taste. A budget was set first: the two new layers
should land between 2.0 and 4.0 points of Commissionable Volume above the live
plan's 14.6085 on a stated month. The draft rates were then measured against it and
scaled once by a stated rule.

- Draft rates of 4 percent, 2 percent and 5 percent paid **21.9712 percent** of
  Commissionable Volume, an increment of 7.3627 points, far outside the budget.
- The one-shot rule applied a single common factor of 3.0 / 7.3627 = 0.4075, giving
  **1.5 percent, 1.0 percent and 2.0 percent**, each rounded to the nearest half of
  a tenth of a percent.
- The calibrated result was **18.9399 percent**, an increment of **4.3314 points,
  which is 0.33 of a point ABOVE the budget's ceiling.**

The miss has a cause worth naming: proration relief is not linear. As rates shrink,
fewer orders reach their cap (orders with a binding override factor fell from 333 to
120), so the payout shrinks more slowly than the rates do.

**That residue was accepted and displayed rather than tuned away.** Iterating the
rates to convergence would have been solving a fixed point against the ceiling's own
proration, which is fitting a number rather than measuring one. The budget was a
budget, not a law, and the miss is printed here for the same reason the inversion is.

Two more things the calibration produced, both published:

- **A prediction of ours was falsified and corrected.** The specification expected
  the real population to be SPARSER in nested Builders than the small teaching
  fixture, so the increment should have landed lower. It landed far higher. The
  sentence was corrected to the measured truth rather than defended.
- **A counter-intuitive redistribution.** Shrinking the SENIOR rates made the JUNIOR
  layer's total pay RISE, from 287.78 to 367.49, because smaller senior claims leave
  the junior class more room under the ceiling. That was verified independently as
  pure waterfall arithmetic, not a defect.

### 11. How this was checked before anybody saw it

The plan was not presentable until three independent gates passed it, each equal in
standing.

**Arithmetic gate: PASS.** An independent implementation recomputed the entire
1,001-member month from raw data with its own level map, its own ranks, its own
group volumes, its own override walk, its own leg ranking, its own per-order claims
and its own truncated factors, and matched the engine on every line in both
directions: zero differences on level pay, zero on overrides, zero on bonuses, zero
invariant violations across 1,001 orders, and the same total of 17,417.12 to the
cent. Zero high-severity findings. That gate also built three hostile cases of its
own design, including the repeating-decimal factor and the fully exhausted pool
described in section 6, and the ceiling held in all three.

**Quality gate: PASS.**

**Red-team gate: initially NOT PRESENTABLE.** A reviewer briefed as a veteran
compensation consultant and told to make the plan fail before anybody saw it ran
eight lines of attack in the live engine. Seven held. One broke a claim the plan
document made, which is the inversion in section 5 above. The reviewer also caught
the document quoting rates its own parameter table had superseded, and required
three number-pairings for any presentation, which this brief follows. The plan was
amended the same day, the reviewer re-inspected item by item against their own
required list, and the gate flipped to PASS.

Two further properties on the record. Two independent implementations of this plan
must agree to the cent, and they do. And the same month computed twice produces the
same ordered set of lines and the same digest.
