Flow 1 - the whole thing

End-to-end payment

The five zones of a payment: the shopper starts it, our neutral vault turns the card into a token, the routing core picks a processor, the processor approves it, and we settle and report. The card never enters Orvanna itself. Watch a payment run all the way through.

Press Run a payment.

How it works

  • Five zones: Initiation, Vault, Orchestrator, Provider, Settlement.
  • The vault owns the raw card. The orchestrator only ever sees tokens.
  • A decline loops back to the routing core to retry or reroute.
  • A response path tells the shopper the result; settlement reconciles the money.

Why this matters for the investor

  • Card data sits in a separate vault, so Orvanna stays out of the heaviest PCI scope.
  • One clean path serves every market. Add a market without redrawing this picture.
  • Every box here is a model we have already designed, not a hope.
Flow 2 - own the card

Vault and the token we own

The first time a shopper pays, their card goes into a neutral vault we control, and a network token is created under OUR name. That is what makes the token portable: we can hand the same token to any processor, and switch processors without ever re-collecting a card.

Run it
Live log
Press Store a card.

How it works

  • The PAN (real card number) is stored only in the neutral vault.
  • The network token is provisioned under OUR token-requestor control, so it is portable.
  • At charge time the token is tried first; the card is the backup.
  • The networks keep the token fresh when the card is reissued.

Why this matters for the investor

  • We own our payment data. No processor can hold our tokens hostage.
  • Switching or adding a processor needs zero card re-collection.
  • The token must be ours, not the acquirer's. That is the make-or-break we verify with each vault.
Flow 3 - authenticate

3DS, standalone and pluggable

When a card or a region requires it, the shopper is authenticated by their bank first (3DS), then the payment routes to the best processor. The 3DS provider is a plug-in: swap it and nothing else in the flow changes. Recurring charges are usually exempt, which matters a lot for subscriptions.

Run it
Live log
Press a scenario.

How it works

  • Authenticate first (Model A), then route to the best processor.
  • Frictionless passes silently; a challenge asks the shopper to confirm.
  • The authentication result rides along into the authorization.
  • The 3DS provider is swappable, like a connector.

Why this matters for the investor

  • We meet regional mandates without being locked to one 3DS vendor.
  • A passed challenge can shift fraud liability to the bank.
  • Subscriptions are not dragged through 3DS on every renewal.
Flow 4 - our edge

Honest retry

Every orchestrator retries declines. Ours is honest: it fights hard for payments that are actually recoverable, and it refuses to waste attempts and fees on customers who are already gone.

Run it
Live log
Press a scenario.

How it works

  • Soft decline (timeout, do-not-honor): retry token, then card, then another processor.
  • Hard decline (stolen, closed account): stop at once, no retry.
  • Dead subscription (past the failed-streak limit): stop, unrecoverable.
  • A charge-once key means recovery can never double-bill.

Why this matters for the investor

  • Recovery is real money, not a vanity number.
  • We do not burn fees or annoy banks chasing the unrecoverable.
  • This honesty layer is original to us, built on our own dead-sub analysis.
Flow 5 - grow coverage

Connect a processor

Adding a processor is not a custom project, it is a repeatable job. Build one small adapter to our standard interface, run it through certification, and it goes live. This is what makes "add a market" a known, priceable unit of work.

Run it
Live log
Press Build and certify.

How it works

  • The core speaks one interface: authorize, capture, refund, void, tokenize, status.
  • Each processor gets one thin adapter that translates that interface to its API.
  • Certification (sandbox) must pass before any live traffic.
  • Adapters are sourced from the open core, reference libraries, or the processor docs.

Why this matters for the investor

  • Coverage grows predictably, a known cost per processor.
  • No vendor lock-in: we own every adapter we build.
  • "Add a market" becomes a business line, not a mystery.
Flow 6 - prove the money

Reconciliation, for us and the merchant

Approved is not the same as paid. Reconciliation matches three independent records, our ledger, the processor's settlement file, and the actual bank deposit, until they agree. Anything that does not agree drops into an exception queue a human works. One engine, two views: the platform (us) and the merchant.

Run it
Live log
Press Match (clean).

How it works

  • Three-way match: our event ledger, the processor settlement file, and the bank deposit.
  • Agree within tolerance, tie-out clean. Disagree, it becomes an exception in the console.
  • Checked at three levels: each transaction, each payout, and the period close.
  • The engine is its own subsystem; the console is just where a human works the exceptions.

Why this matters for the investor

  • One reconciled view across every processor is a headline reason a merchant switches.
  • Our own books tie out, so recovery and approval claims are provable, not asserted.
  • We adopt HyperSwitch's recon module and add the bank leg, not a build from scratch.
