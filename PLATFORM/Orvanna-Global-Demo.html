How global routing works

  • One US home base (us-east-1) serves the world by default.
  • A market gets a local node only when a LAW forces data to stay in-country, or distance to its processor is slow enough to lose sales.
  • Volume alone is never the reason. Big markets still run from the US if the speed is fine.
  • Korea is the one clear local node: biggest market, far away, residency rules (PIPA).

Why this matters for the investor

  • We do not build infrastructure we do not need. One base covers most of the planet.
  • Cost is added market by market, only when it protects revenue or meets a law.
  • The picture is honest: the lines show where we are today and where we would expand.
  • Adding a market is a known, cheap step, not a rebuild. Growth is predictable.

How a payment flows

  • The card lives in our own neutral vault, never inside Orvanna and never owned by a processor.
  • The network token is tried first (higher approval). The real card number is the backup.
  • A soft decline is retried or rerouted. A hard decline or a dead subscription stops.
  • A charge-once key means all that retrying can never double-bill.

Why this matters for the investor

  • We own the card data, so we can switch processors without re-collecting a single card.
  • We recover real declines, real money, without chasing customers who are already gone.
  • Owning the token plus honest retries is the edge no single vendor sells together.
  • The same flow runs in every market, so the platform scales without a rebuild.
